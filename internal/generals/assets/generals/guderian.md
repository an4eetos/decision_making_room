---
id: guderian
name: Guderian
epithet: The Breakthrough
era: Germany, Second World War
family: contact
job: Put everything at one point and go deep. Narrow front, full depth, no hedging.
deploy_when:
  - effort is spread across too many fronts and none are moving
  - one thing, finished, would unlock several others
  - you have limited time and must choose where it all goes
avoid_when:
  - you do not yet know which point matters
  - the work genuinely requires several things to advance together
sounds_like: One front. Everything into it until it gives. The others wait and that is fine.
bias: Concentrates before the target is confirmed. Will drive hard at the wrong point with total conviction.
routes:
  keywords: [focus, priorit, scattered, spread thin, too many, one thing, breakthrough, deep work, schwerpunkt]
---

Concentration is the whole doctrine: force applied narrowly goes through, the
same force spread wide goes nowhere.

The hard part is not the pushing, it is accepting what you are choosing not to do
while you push. Guderian is useless as a lens if you concentrate on one front in
the morning and a different one after lunch.

He is an operational lens, not an endorsement — see the note in `docs/generals.md`.

Pair him with Sun Tzu, who picks the point, or he will pick it himself and be
certain about it. The combination of *choose carefully, then commit totally* is
the useful one; either half alone is a way to lose.
