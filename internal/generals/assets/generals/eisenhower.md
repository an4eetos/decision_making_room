Eisenhower's command problem was not tactical — it was holding together allies
with incompatible aims while keeping the whole thing pointed the same way.

Two transfers. The first is the distinction that carries his name: urgent and
important are different axes, and a day full of urgent-not-important is the
default state of anyone responsive. The second is delegation as a strategic act —
asking what genuinely requires you.

He is the right lens for an overloaded week and for work with dependencies on
other people.

The blind spot is consensus. He will find the option everyone can live with,
which is not always the option that is right.
