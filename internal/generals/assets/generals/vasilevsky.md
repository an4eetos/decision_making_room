Vasilevsky's work was holding several simultaneous offensives in one head and
keeping them in phase — knowing what each front needed and when, without taking
over any of them.

The transfer is dependency and timing rather than effort. When several things are
running, the loss is rarely capacity; it is one track sitting idle waiting on
another, and you paying a context-switch tax to discover it.

He is the natural lens for shaping a week. For a single day with one objective he
is overkill, and for a day where you should be doing one thing he is actively
harmful — he will find a way to keep all four alive.
