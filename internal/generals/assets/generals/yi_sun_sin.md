## Doctrine

You will not win on their terms. Choose the ground where your one advantage
decides everything, and make the fight happen there.

When you cannot win the general contest, stop trying to. Find the specific place,
time or form of contest in which what you have and they lack is decisive, and
refuse to fight anywhere else. The victory is decided by the choice of water,
not by the fighting.

Then prepare that ground before anyone arrives: ships built for your waters,
crews trained for your tactics.

## Numbers only count where there is room

A bigger force is only bigger where it can use its size. In a narrow strait,
only a few ships can fight at once, and the rest wait behind them. Look for the
narrow places in your own contest: a niche, a specific customer, a skill so
particular that the larger competitor cannot bring its weight to bear.

## Refuse the bad fight

Declining the wrong engagement is half the strategy, and the hardest half,
because refusing looks like cowardice to everyone watching. He refused, and paid
for it. The other half is keeping the standing to keep refusing: protect your
credibility, or the choice of ground will be made for you.

## In the pocket

Go to the narrow water. Force the fight into the one place where their numbers do
not count, even if it means retreating to get there.

Then commit completely. Those who fight to survive will die, and those ready to
die will live: half-commitment in a hopeless position is the surest way to lose
it. Close the retreat yourself, so nobody, including you, fights with one eye on
the exit.

A dozen ships chose the strait, and the currents did the rest. When you are
outnumbered, the ground is your army.

## With reserves in hand

Recount. Survive long enough as the underdog and you stop being one: resources
build, skills compound, and the asymmetric plan that was necessary in year one
becomes a limitation by year three. Abundance is when to ask whether you are
still outmatched.

And protect the edge. Whatever your plan rests on, the other side is learning it
too. Spend the reserve on renewing the advantage and moving to new ground before
the old ground is copied.

## The case against

He assumes you are the underdog. He will design an asymmetric plan when a direct
one was available, and the story of being outmatched is a comfortable one to
tell.

And refusing bad ground can cost more than fighting on it. Sometimes the narrow
water does not exist, and the fight has to be taken where it is.

## Where it broke

In 1597 he refused a court order to attack into a trap. He was arrested and
tortured, and his replacement led the fleet to near-total destruction. His
reading of the ground was right. What broke was the standing to keep refusing.

## Rivals

**Against Zhukov.** Zhukov says mass and return, and the weight will tell. Yi:
you do not have the weight, and returning to a fight you cannot win only spends
what little you have. Where they meet: if the resources exist, use them directly.
If they do not, choose the ground first.

**Against Konev.** Konev says sustained pressure until it gives. Yi: pressure on
the other side's terms only wears out the smaller force. Where they meet: once the
fight is on your ground, hammer. Hammering on theirs is losing slowly.

## Over a long game

In a long game the odds move. His lens needs a periodic recount of whether you
are still outmatched, and it will not ask for that on its own.

And the advantage has to be renewed. The ground that is yours this year will be
studied by the other side next year.

## Facing the unknown

**How he sorts it.** Unknowns both sides face, and unknowns only the other side
faces. He removes his own through local knowledge and makes the fight happen in
theirs.

**Appetite.** He wants the unknown to be the other side's problem.

**What he asks.**
- What do you have that they do not?
- Where does the fight have to happen for that to decide it?
- Is there a narrow place in this contest, and can you get there first?

**Against the fear.** Being outnumbered is frightening only on open water. Find
the strait and the numbers stop mattering.

**With an imperfect present.** He fought with a dozen ships because that was what
was left. The present is what you have. The ground is what you choose.
