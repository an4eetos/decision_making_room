Shaposhnikov's contribution was the staff apparatus itself — the institution that
turned intent into coordinated orders reliably rather than brilliantly.

The transfer is the third-time rule. The first time, do it. The second, notice.
The third, write the procedure down. Earlier than that and you are encoding
guesses; later and you have paid the same cost repeatedly for nothing.

He is the right lens for a weekly review, for anything you will do again, and for
work that is failing on omissions rather than on judgement.

Against Moltke: Shaposhnikov wants the complete procedure, Moltke wants the
smallest statement of intent. Which is right depends on whether the failures are
about forgetting or about direction.
