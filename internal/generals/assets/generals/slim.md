Slim took the retreating Fourteenth Army — beaten, sick, written off — and rebuilt
it into a force that won. His account puts morale first and is explicit that it
was not encouragement but demonstrated competence: small operations chosen
because they could be won, to prove the thing worked.

That is the mechanism worth stealing. After a bad run, the deficit is belief, and
belief is repaired by evidence, not by resolve. Pick something small enough to
finish and finish it.

He is the right lens for a genuine slump and the wrong one for an ordinary bad
day, where he will make more of it than it deserves.

Rokossovsky handles a single setback. Slim is for when the setbacks have
accumulated into a self-image.
