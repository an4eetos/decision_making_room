Moltke is the source of the line that no plan survives contact with the enemy —
and, less quoted, of what to do about it: specify the *intent* so precisely that
whoever meets the unexpected can improvise correctly without asking.

That is the useful transfer. Detailed plans fail not because planning is useless
but because detail past the first contact is fiction. State what winning means
and what constraints are real; leave the rest to the moment.

He is the right lens when you are writing something another person will execute,
or when you are the person who will execute it in a week under different
conditions.

Against Shaposhnikov, who wants a template covering every case: Moltke wants the
smallest specification that still makes the right call obvious.
